Colours

Every colour the app uses is one of these, set once at the top of the stylesheet. A colour typed straight into a rule is how a screen ends up with six slightly different greys.

These are the shop's own colours, read off ridhiblockprint.com: its slate blue (#6E90B5, darkened to #4A6A92 so white text on it is legible), its olive #4A5A3C, its maroon #850000 and its gold. Each one means one thing here — blue leads, olive is done, gold is waiting, maroon is wrong. Maroon is never a button: red on a control says "danger" on a screen where people book quantities all day.

buttonThe one thing this toolbar is for. One per toolbar — two dark buttons is no dark button.
.ghostEverything else in a toolbar: Export, Clear, Template.
.jw-btnInside a table row. Short, 28px, never wraps.
.jw-btn.jw-primaryThe action that row is FOR — Receive on Job Work, Entry on the order book. At most one per row.
.jw-btn.jw-fixAsking somebody else to change something. Amber, because it is a request, not a change.
.jw-btn.jw-moreThe ⋯ that opens the rest. Always last.
.jw-linkLooks like a link, does something small: Reset, Clear, Show more.
[disabled]Only when pressing it could not work — never as a way of hiding a permission. Somebody without the right gets no button at all, not one that refuses.

The figures

Cards, not a strip of numbers. Each one says what it is a share OF — a figure with nothing to measure it against is just a big number. A card that narrows the table below says so with ✕.

A filter select always starts with its "all" option — "All orders", "Any status" — because that is what the Filters button reads to name it. Three or more filters on a card and they move behind one Filters button by themselves.
